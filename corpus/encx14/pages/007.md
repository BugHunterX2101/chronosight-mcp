## 7.1 Control loop timing

The joint controller usually runs at 1 kHz. Each control period it reads
the angle, computes the motor command and writes the PWM duty. The read
takes a known time and the value returned is older than the moment of the
read; see the latency table on page 12.

## 7.2 Derivative term

A PD or PID loop differentiates the measured angle. Differentiation
amplifies high-frequency noise. With the FILT filter off, the raw output
noise of 12 LSB RMS becomes a large velocity noise at a 1 kHz loop rate
(page 48). This noise drives the motor, heats it, and can excite the
mechanical resonance of a light arm. Page 49 explains how to choose a FILT
setting for this application.

## 7.3 Brown-out in a joint

Joint boards often share a supply with the motor driver. A motor current
spike can pull the 3.3 V rail below VBOR (2.6 V) for a few microseconds.
That causes a brown-out reset, which clears CONF2 (page 58). The joint
keeps running, but with the filter off. Page 53 describes how to detect
this.
