# 48 Quantisation and noise in a derivative term

## 48.1 Why the derivative path matters

A PD or PID position loop, and every velocity loop, uses the difference of
two successive angle samples. Differencing multiplies white noise by
sqrt(2) and divides it by the sample period. At a 1 kHz loop rate, small
angle noise becomes large velocity noise.

## 48.2 Velocity noise versus FILT (1 kHz loop)

Table 48-1. Noise of the one-step difference (angle[k] - angle[k-1]) / 1 ms.

  FILT n   Angle noise      Velocity noise      Velocity noise
           (LSB RMS)        (LSB per ms RMS)    (° / s RMS)
  0        12.0             17.0                373
  1        6.93             9.8                 215
  2        4.54             6.4                 141
  3        3.10             3.6                 78
  5        1.51             0.8                 17
  7        0.75             0.2                 4

For FILT >= 3 successive 1 ms samples are correlated by the filter, so the
velocity noise falls faster than the angle noise.

## 48.3 Consequences at FILT = 0

  - A derivative gain that is reasonable for the mechanics multiplies
    373 °/s RMS of noise into the current command.
  - The motor current carries broadband noise. The motor heats, the joint
    buzzes audibly, and a light link can resonate.
  - The error appears as an increase in tracking error and in RMS motor
    current, not as a fault flag. Nothing in STATUS reports it.

## 48.4 Quantisation

At 16384 counts per turn and a 1 kHz loop, one LSB per sample corresponds
to 22 °/s. Quantisation is small compared with the noise at FILT <= 3 and
can be ignored for loop design.
