# 49 Choosing the FILT setting

## 49.1 The trade-off

A larger FILT lowers the noise (table 31-2, table 48-1) and adds phase lag
(table 12-1). The right value depends on the loop crossover frequency.

## 49.2 Phase lag at the crossover

Table 49-1. Total encoder phase lag (2 ms read delay plus FILT lag).

  FILT n   tau       Lag at 4 Hz   Lag at 10 Hz
  0        off       2.9°          7.2°
  3        1 ms      4.3°          10.8°
  5        4 ms      8.6°          21.3°
  7        16 ms     24.8°         52.4°

The values at 4 Hz match table 12-1.

## 49.3 Recommendation for robot joints

For a joint with a 1 kHz loop and a crossover between 3 Hz and 10 Hz:

  - FILT = 3 (CONF2 = 0x03) is the recommended starting point. It reduces
    the angle noise by a factor of 3.9 and the velocity noise by a factor
    of almost 5, and adds about 1.4° of lag at 4 Hz.
  - FILT = 0 is not recommended. The loop is stable, but the derivative
    path drives the motor with noise (page 48).
  - FILT >= 5 is only for slow loops. FILT = 7 costs about 25° at 4 Hz and
    can make a loop that was stable with FILT = 0 oscillate.

## 49.4 FILT must be maintained

The FILT setting chosen here is lost on every reset, including brown-out
resets that the host does not otherwise see (page 11, page 58). Choosing
FILT is half of the job; the other half is restoring it whenever
STATUS.RST = 1 (page 45).
