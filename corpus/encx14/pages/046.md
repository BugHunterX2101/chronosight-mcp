# 46 OTP programming

## 46.1 What OTP stores

OTP holds CONF1, ZPOS and MPOS (page 29, page 30). After every reset these
registers are reloaded from OTP. CONF2 is not stored in OTP (page 36).

## 46.2 Production procedure

  1. Mount the magnet and complete mechanical assembly.
  2. Supply VDD between 3.2 V and 3.6 V (errata E-1, page 57). On rev 0xA2
     silicon 3.0 V is sufficient, but 3.3 V is recommended for all parts.
  3. Read CHIP_ID (0x14) and REV. Record REV in the production log.
  4. Move the joint to its mechanical zero and read RAW (0x0E-0x0F).
  5. Write ZPOS = RAW. If the joint travels less than a full turn, move to
     the end stop, read RAW and write MPOS.
  6. Write CONF1 if a non-default power mode or ADCSLOW is required.
  7. Write 0x80 to CMD (0x30). Wait at least 10 ms without bus access.
  8. Verify the burn as described on page 36.

## 46.3 When not to program OTP

Many robot designs keep ZPOS and MPOS at their defaults and apply the zero
offset in host firmware. This avoids a production step that can be done
only once and lets a joint be re-zeroed after repair. The configuration
that matters for servo loops, CONF2, cannot be programmed in OTP in any
case and must be written by the host after every reset.

## 46.4 Failure modes

  - Burn at VDD below 3.2 V on rev 0xA1: partial burn, ZPOS and MPOS read
    back with random bits (errata E-1).
  - Bus access during the 10 ms burn: the burn completes, but the access is
    NACKed and must be repeated.
  - A second burn command is ignored. There is no way to erase OTP.
