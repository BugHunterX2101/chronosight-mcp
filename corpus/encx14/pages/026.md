# 26 Noise sources

## 26.1 Noise breakdown at FILT = 0, 60 mT, 25 °C

  Source                              Contribution (LSB RMS)
  Hall plate thermal noise            9.8
  Front-end amplifier noise           6.2
  ADC quantisation                    2.4
  Supply-coupled noise (with 4.7 µF)  1.1
  CORDIC rounding                     0.3
  Total (root sum of squares)         12.0

## 26.2 Spectrum

With FILT = 0 the noise is white from DC to 4 kHz (half the 8 kHz sampling
rate). A host that samples the angle at 1 kHz sees the whole 0 to 4 kHz
noise power aliased into its 0 to 500 Hz band. The RMS value per read is
therefore still 12 LSB.

## 26.3 Effect of FILT

FILT is a first-order low-pass filter running at 8 kHz. It reduces the
bandwidth of the white noise and therefore its RMS value. The noise gain of
the filter is sqrt(alpha / (2 - alpha)), where alpha = 2^-n (page 31).

## 26.4 Dependence on field

  Field     Noise multiplier
  30 mT     1.20
  45 mT     1.08
  60 mT     1.00
  90 mT     0.95

## 26.5 Dependence on temperature

The noise increases by about 0.1 % per °C above 25 °C, so by about 10 % at
125 °C.

## 26.6 Noise is not drift

Noise as specified here is the fast, random part of the error. Slow
variations with temperature or shaft angle are covered on page 27.
